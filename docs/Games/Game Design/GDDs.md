Public and design guidelines

Who's this game for?

We decided on a broad age range audience (+10 years old) of casual players who enjoy Indie games, seek level exploration with a touch of battle. Users who are interested in immersive story-driven games and are willing to spend their time uncovering every possible piece of story in this world, also seeking to alternate the slower parts, such as exploration and dialogue, with more exciting combat and/or faster segments like runner moments.

Why do we want to reach this audience?

According to a brief research we conducted, the audience today has a strong preference for shooting, fighting, and strategy games.


> Chart showing in a sample of 17 results that people enjoy violent games.

What we aim to do is break away from this trend and bring something fresh to the gaming industry that doesn't involve gratuitous violence and instead offers something more sincere and captivating, while also imparting values to the player that we believe are lacking in the world.

How will we reach this target audience?

- Since the audience is diverse, we're pursuing a distinctive art style that employs 2.5D graphics, utilizing gameplay layouts and textures reminiscent of paintings with a "brushstroke" effect. We'll use more saturated colors and incorporate intriguing characters. The goal is to not only attract a younger audience but also those who are interested in unique artistic elements in games, especially those who enjoy exploring the game universe to appreciate it as an artistic work.
- We're also aiming to engage them through an emotional, enjoyable, and profound storyline that appeals to all ages while also bringing forth questions, values, and learning opportunities. We'll introduce innovative mechanics for exploration, puzzles, and defensive combat, avoiding the concept of direct conflict while still making the game exciting and enjoyable.
- What is the gaming market lacking for this audience, and how will we address it?
  - The current gaming market for children and teenagers lacks cooperative games with a deep storyline. While there are shooting and adventure games available, they often prioritize gameplay over the story. Our game aims to bring a cooperative storyline that resonates with older audiences while also teaching concepts of friendship, loyalty, trust, and genuine collaboration between two entirely different characters. We will create moments of thrilling action and mystery, as well as captivating moments that draw players closer to the story we want to tell and the world we've created.
  - We don't just aim to engage players with the story; we also challenge them with new mechanics for cooperation, platforming, puzzles, and combat. We'll introduce moments of enjoyable experimentation, drawing inspiration from classic games in our repertoire to update and creatively combine interesting mechanics.
- What are the inspirations for this game?
  - Tomba - PS1
  - Super Mario Odyssey - Switch
  - Donkey Kong Country Returns (or Tropical Freeze) - Wii
  - Odin Sphere - PS2
  - Prinny 2 - PS2
  - Dark Cloud 2 - PS2
  - Breath of Fire IV - PSX
- Project Scope 
  - Before proceeding with the full project, we've decided to adapt all the key features of the game, such as map progression, puzzles, dialogue, cooperative mechanics, and spline gameplay, into a more streamlined scenario. This scenario will incorporate all the mechanics together for testing and adaptations, serving as a kind of tutorial demo to showcase the game.

World building

The game takes place in a world that has reached a technological level equivalent to the 1850s. However, only a few isolated cities exist. The primary mode of transportation for travel is the train, which connects most of the city-states.

The majority of the game is set in forests, plains, caves, ruins, and valleys, with minimal contact with these cities. Explorable parts of the game feature ruins and remnants of other civilizations, whether from other animals or ancient human civilizations. These ruins play an important role in the game's narrative and development. The goal is to evoke a mystical feeling while navigating through each environment, as supernatural forces are integral to the game's story.

Synopsis

- In the depths of a forest, within a ruin, something mysterious awakens dormant forces, causing an initial earthquake.
- A circus train traveling between cities is affected by the tremor, causing one of the animal wagons to fall off a bridge into the forest. This wagon, in reality, holds a Tiger within its cage.
- A rabbit wandering nearby, drawn by the commotion, discovers the Tiger's cage and, after initial reluctance and conflicts, decides to help it.
- As the rabbit decides to help the tiger, a loose piece of the bridge falls onto the cage, crushing its structure and creating a large enough hole for the tiger to escape. With the rabbit startled and the tiger still lost and disoriented, they both find themselves having to work together despite their differences and distinct goals. They must align to achieve what they want, turning this initially undesired alliance — the Tiger wanting to return home and the Rabbit wanting to save its home — into the only way to accomplish their goals: understanding what caused the earthquake.
- As they journey deeper into the forest, they sense a chilling aura in the environment and encounter strange "moving rocks." Following the trail of these peculiar beings, they discover an unknown ruin deep within the forest. Here, they confront a gigantic stone golem, the ancient guardian of the place. It had been asleep but was awakened by the presence of an evil energy in the temple, distorting it. It's here that the characters realize the gravity of their encounter. Regaining consciousness, the golem explains the situation to them and recognizes the ancient lineage of protectors from the Rabbit. It asks the "heroes" to aid the other awakened elemental guardians in breaking free. Can they overcome their differences to fight together for their world in this mission?

Story

- The story can be divided into three acts:
  - Act 1 
    - The Rabbit and the Tiger learn to coexist, an uncomfortable situation for both. The Rabbit must cooperate with a predator to return to his village and survive, while the Tiger is in an unfamiliar place and completely disoriented. Some pilot scenes for this act:
    - Scene 01
      - After hearing a noise and investigating, the Rabbit finds some fallen crates near a train bridge. He discovers that one of these crates is actually a Tiger's cage. The Tiger asks for help upon seeing the Rabbit. The Rabbit is hesitant to open the cage for a predator that could devour him instantly, even though the Tiger swears he would never do that. With a moral dilemma, the Rabbit decides whether to open the Tiger's cage or not, and as he reaches for the key, a stone falls on the cage, opening it. Despite the Rabbit claiming he would have opened it, the Tiger is never entirely sure.
    - Scene 02
      - While attempting to cross an obstacle, the Rabbit realizes only the Tiger can do so. Initially refusing to go along, the Rabbit changes his mind when the Tiger can't progress and convinces him to proceed together.
    - Scene 03
      - They encounter a strange creature and avoid confrontation.
    - Scene 04
      - During a pause, perhaps when they decide to rest and reorient themselves due to the changing path ahead, they have a conversation. The Tiger shares about his life in the circus and his desire to return "home." The Rabbit expresses concern about his village, as much has changed and strange events are occurring. Conflict scenes can be added gradually to develop the characters' relationship.
    - Scene ?? 
      - A quadrupedal golem appears, frightening the Rabbit. However, he remembers he has the Tiger by his side, which reassures him. Yet, when the golem grows in size after being defeated and the Rabbit sees the Tiger's inability to stop it, he flees with the Tiger.
    - Scene ?? 
      - A crucial point in the character change occurs. The Tiger opens up about his past, revealing that his circus life wasn't as good as he remembered or liked to recall.
    - Scene ??
      - They encounter a Wolf in the forest who is surprised by the presence of the Tiger, an uncommon sight in those parts, especially accompanied by a Rabbit. The Wolf jokingly suggests that the Tiger should eat the Rabbit or let the Wolf eat him, but ultimately asks for help in finding food.
    - Scene ??
      - An animal is kidnapped by a golem in the protagonists' presence, prompting them to follow the trail and rescue it.
  - Act 2
    - After learning the origin of the strange occurrences, the Rabbit discovers that much of what he knew has changed or been destroyed. Both characters are forced to venture out. The Rabbit, seeking to prove his worth, sees his chance, but realizes he has no chance alone. The Tiger, after experiencing freedom, the forest, and its inhabitants, begins to rethink returning to the circus.
  - Act 3
    - After a grueling battle against the last golem, the Tiger is thrown and knocked unconscious by a powerful impact. Desperate and distraught, the Rabbit rushes to his side, confirming he's alive. With tears in his eyes, the Rabbit turns to the golem and enters battle alone, unsure of the outcome. After a challenging battle, the golem crumbles into inanimate objects as if it had never been a living being. However, the Artifact's energy still shines brightly, and the Rabbit's friend lies unconscious on the ground, taking his final breath. The Rabbit decides to use the magic that he had been combating throughout his journey, merging it with his friend, who's on the brink of death. This fusion propels the Rabbit away, with the energy now radiating from both. The Tiger, now floating as if levitating, draws nearby ruins to him. With a brilliant flash, the Tiger becomes a golem but unlike the others. The Rabbit senses his friend's presence within and realizes an internal struggle between the Artifact's magic and the Tiger's essence. The Rabbit understands he must fight his best friend to free him.
    - a. Rabbit Wins: After a harrowing battle, the Rabbit finally releases his friend, shattering the Artifact's power. The Tiger, now separated from the Artifact's influence, remains unharmed, cured by the magic's miracle. Together, inseparable friends, they return to the Rabbit's village, where they confront his family, gaining recognition for their achievements and friendship. The Rabbit becomes a humble guardian of the village, no longer seeking glory. Together, they protect the village and help it flourish.
    - b. The Rabbit fuses with the Tiger, becoming an even larger golem. This fusion allows them to harness the Artifact's power for themselves. They become guardians of the environment, protecting the region.

Characters

- Main
  - Tiger
    - 
      > Tiger concept art by Victor Perrotte
      > 
    - Born into the circus, he always had a life of performances and training. Despite being itinerant, his world is confined, and contact with others and the external world was never allowed. His best friends have always been other circus animals and a few select humans. Never having experienced anything better, he feels comfortable and secure to the point of fearing losing it all. The concept of freedom has never crossed his mind, as he's never known any reality other than the one he lives in. Hearing about bigger and more famous circuses, he dreams of being a part of these grand spectacles.
    - His fondest memories are of his first performance, receiving applause, and playing with other animals at the camp. However, not all experiences in his life have been pleasant. He carries suppressed memories of his training and the suffering he witnessed some animals, including his mother, endure in the circus. Despite loving attention, his greatest joy is making others happy. He often becomes playful as a means to avoid discussing old memories and more complex emotions.
    - Influenced by his circus life, he is extremely naive and ignorant. He despises boredom and sometimes reacts in exaggerated ways. This can be confusing to others, but he exudes confidence, even when unsure.
  - Rabbit
    - 
      > Rabbit Concept art by Victor Perrotte
      > 
    - The youngest child of an important family, he lives in a village that houses various types of animals. His role there is to observe the surroundings, ensuring everything is safe and normal. Despite being highly creative and intelligent, he's consistently underestimated by his family. He's responsible, idealistic, and very friendly, even though he occasionally enjoys isolation.
    - Although lacking courage, he thoroughly enjoys investigating the unknown and being in control of situations.
    - His memories of playing with friends in the woods, feeling freedom, and relishing the beauty of the landscapes are very positive. However, he has suffered considerably while trying to prove his worth, which occasionally translates into his perfectionism and insecurity.
- Bossess
  - The main enemies in the game will be Golems, each located at the end of every environment. The battle against them will differ from battles against other enemies. There will be one Golem per environment, each representing a distinct element. These Golems are ancient world guardians, created by an ancient civilization to maintain natural balance and prevent the unrestrained destruction that human development could bring about. With the emergence of this mysterious energy and its influence on the world, the guardians abruptly awaken from their slumber. Weakened and influenced by the malevolent force of this enigmatic artifact, they are now uncontrollable. Your task is to aid these guardians in regaining their sanity.
  - Forest Golem - The Boulder
    -

Furious, the guardian sends its minions into the forest to investigate what's happening. Having remained dormant for an extended period, its powers have weakened over time. Ultimately, it becomes influenced by the malevolent force, turning aggressive and posing a danger even to the very forest it is meant to safeguard. Likely the eldest of all elemental guardians, it could potentially offer significant aid on the journey, but first, it needs to be cleansed of its malevolent influence.

    - The guardian is deeply attentive to nature and the animals living within its forest. It recognizes the Rabbit and its family's devotion to protecting the forest, expressing gratitude and tranquility while offering its assistance to the heroes. However, it finds the Tiger's more relaxed behavior strange, considering the Tiger's background from the circus and limited understanding of wildlife.
- Enemies
  - Enemies will consist of the golem's minions, which will be like "extensions" of the golems that have spread throughout the environment to exert control. Additionally, there will be dangerous animals such as wolves, which will lead to chase scenes or quests that culminate in battles against them.
- Other Characters
  - Rescued animals will be those that have suffered in some way due to the changes caused by the artifact. These animals would come from the Rabbit's village, offering quests to the main characters. Upon completion, these quests will yield rewards for the protagonists and contribute to the gradual reconstruction of the village.

Scenarios

- Environments
  - 1st Environment: Home - The Rabbit's Hole (Forest - Rabbit's Home)
    - This environment is where the less civilized animals reside, and it's considered dangerous by those in the city. However, the truth is that there is no actual danger; instead, there is freedom. The animals living there have different cultures and appear wiser than the "civilized" animals. This forest is different, infused with a kind of magic in the air—a benevolent magic that brings tranquility and joy to all who frequent it. The forest features vast green fields and open spaces for free-roaming animals. There are few dense vegetation areas and tall trees that block out the sky, along with ruins consumed by nature, with vegetation concealing structures that once stood there. These distinct locations also serve as navigational landmarks, preventing explorers from getting lost. This is the typical state of things.
    - However, with the revelation of the artifact and the shift in magical energy, the forest turns into a genuinely perilous place. The plains and open fields where animals once roamed freely now become exposed pathways for an enigmatic adversary to capture them with ease. Dense forested areas that were good for hiding now turn dark, almost void of light, making it difficult to escape. The greatest problem lies within the ruins. The closer you get, the darker it becomes, and the forest takes on an ominous air. There's a sense of malevolence emanating from this place. It's said that a fragment of the artifact is there or a portal to a place closer to it or perhaps even to the source of this energy, which might not even be the artifact itself but rather one of the monsters this magic has awakened.
  - 2nd Environment: Underground - The Unknown Darkness (Caves - Unexplored Darkness)
    - This is a place of obscurity. Pockets of light are illuminated only by moonlight beams reflecting off the aquatic caves. Breathing here is difficult due to limited airflow, and the space is narrow for most of the cave's length. However, there are vast galleries that provide a sense of calm, a certain tranquility. But noise never ceases in this place. Whether it's the drip-drip of water from colossal stalactites almost merging with stalagmites on the ground or bats flying around and emitting sonar calls that echo infinitely through the corridors and galleries of the cave. Despite the noise from the bats, the dripping water creates a soothing sound, like raindrops falling on the ground.
    - Ruins beneath the ground resemble aqueducts of a water supply system, transporting water to cities, and other parts resemble enormous pipes. One pipe appears to lead back to the forest but gives an impression of being unsafe or nonfunctional. It's accompanied by labyrinths of thin, small pipes that seem illogical but follow a certain destination.
    - Upon the arrival of the artifact, things change in this environment. Instead of undergoing a transformation like the forest, the artifact here brings illumination. Near the ruins, an intense light emanates, only blocked by a pile of rocks at its entrance, as it would otherwise illuminate the entire cave. Creatures emerging from this energy provide illumination, dazzling the inhabitants and frightening away the bats. If those rocks were ever removed, this environment would never be the same again.
- Levels
  - Home - Rabbit's Hole Part I: Open Fields
    - This section starts where the tiger's cage/wagon has fallen at the beginning of the level after the first cutscene. Players witness a dialogue between the Tiger and the Rabbit, each claiming they don't need the other. The Tiger sets off and encounters the first puzzle. This puzzle teaches the player how to switch between characters and utilize the Rabbit's mechanics, enabling the Tiger to progress to where the Rabbit is. The roles are reversed soon after, with the Rabbit attempting to proceed and the Tiger assisting in moving forward, involving the second puzzle. This helps them realize that working together improves their chances of progress. Consequently, the third puzzle requires skills learned together, like a long jump. Dialogue between the characters can be interspersed with these puzzles. This might be enough for the initial stages of the game. The setting is an open field with mountains, evoking a sense of freedom and open air. However, as they approach an artifact-affected area, adversaries and darker locations appear, ultimately leading to the dense forest.
  - Home - Rabbit's Hole Part II: The Dense Forest
    - This phase begins in a dimmer location; the forest becomes more enclosed and dense, already affected by the artifact. This stage should be longer than the first. It includes enemies, golems (at least 3) to introduce combat, and somewhat more intricate puzzles compared to the initial puzzles. The story for this area isn't fully fleshed out. As players navigate through these challenges, they reach the entrance of the ruin at the end.
  - Home - Rabbit's Hole Part III: The First Ruins
    - The ruins could feature more geometric puzzles and intricate structures. Puzzles with integrated enemies can be introduced at this point. The setting will include weathered constructions, potentially reminiscent of ancient Aztec temples, like a temple hidden in the forest, covered in vines, with an environment deeply affected by the artifact. There might be a purple particle effect to emphasize this. This stage will culminate in a boss fight against the Stone Golem, who guards the ruins.
- Base
  - The base is a lost and destroyed village in the homeland of the Rabbit, to which he swears to protect and help rebuild.
- Map
  - The map will have a clear division of the Environments and will show the path of all the Levels taken by the player, from the beginning to the current point. The game is linear but has interconnectedness between the ruins.

Mechanics

- Movement
  - The movement mechanics are based on a 2D platforming system, however, there are some distinctive aspects, such as the fact that it is designed within a 3D universe, following straight and even curved paths. Another element is the path-switching feature, adding more depth to the game and significantly expanding possibilities for exploring the environment. This also allows us to avoid the use of "endless pits," as when a player falls into a hole, the penalty is simply having to backtrack along a parallel path and try again. Often, this type of mechanic can also be employed to catch the player by surprise.
- Co-op
  - The idea behind this mechanic is to create a sense of interdependence between the two main characters, a symbiotic relationship. The two will always engage in combat together, solve puzzles with each other's assistance, and overcome obstacles as a team. This dynamic can grow stronger as the game progresses. The intention is for players to feel that the characters learn from one another, showcasing this evolving relationship throughout the game in harmony with the storyline.
- Dialogue System
  - There will be various types of dialogue in the game. Interaction Dialogues, which are short and quick exchanges triggered by minimal interactions, such as attempting to interact with a non-interactive object, trying to access a path the character is not yet able to traverse, or conversations with NPCs offering open quests. Quest Dialogues, used for quest narratives, involve interactions with NPCs and display a portrait of the character the player is interacting with; these dialogues don't block the player's movement and can be skipped using the action button. Main Story Dialogues, on the other hand, are the most complex. They involve extensive exchanges, potentially accompanied by character animations as seen in cutscenes. These dialogues cannot be skipped, but players will have the option to use a "skip story" function accessible through the start/esc button.
  - Interaction Dialogues 
    - Interaction Dialogues are brief and rapid conversations tied to minimal interactions within the game. They might occur when trying to interact with non-interactive objects, attempting to access paths the character is not yet equipped to cross, or conversing with NPCs offering open quests. These dialogues can overlay one another if there are multiple nearby objects triggering them. They are not obstructive, are time-limited, and will disappear after a certain period. These dialogues will be shorter than regular ones and might not include a portrait.
  - Quest Dialogues 
    - Quest Dialogues serve to advance quest-related storylines. They involve interactions with NPCs who will be presented with a character portrait. Importantly, they won't impede player movement and can be swiftly advanced using the action button. Character portraits will change to reflect emotions in sync with the text (more info:).
  - Main Story Dialogues 
    - These dialogues will be the most intricate in the game, encompassing more extended exchanges and potentially incorporating character animations similar to those in cutscenes. These dialogues cannot be skipped outright, but players will have the option to utilize a "skip story" feature by pressing the start/esc button.
- Puzzles
  - The puzzles will be designed in a way that one character assists the other in overcoming obstacles. For instance, a lever only reachable by the Rabbit or a wall that only the Tiger can destroy, both of which enable both characters to progress together. The puzzles will have a similar structure but will be tailored to the theme of the respective Environment.
  - Puzzle Type 1 
    - This puzzle features five buttons scattered on the ground and three nearby stones. When these stones are placed on the buttons, they depress them, altering a nearby balance that affects a door. When the balance is evenly weighted on both sides, the door opens. The objective is to position the stones correctly and use the weight of the characters to adjust the balance.
- Platforms 
  - Platform designs will be in harmony with the theme of each LEVEL, as each level constitutes a segment of the corresponding Environment. Consequently, the platforms will be tailored to match the thematic elements. For instance, LEVEL 2 of Environment 1 could transition from an open field to a dense forest and then proceed from that forest to a swamp, ultimately leading to the entrance of the ruin. Different types of obstacles will be incorporated into each segment of the level to maintain game variety and prevent player fatigue. The transition between segments might be perceptible through shifts in color palettes that darken as the game progresses.
- Combat System
  - Before combat
    - Before combat interaction, enemies will be either at rest (camouflaged as part of the environment) or patrolling (moving back and forth on a platform, for instance).
    - 
  - Combat Start
    - When players enter the enemy's action range, it becomes activated and enters combat mode, attacking the players. In the interface, the visible items are replaced with abilities.
    - 
  - Combat Climax
    - The players' first movement is to dodge the enemy's attack and get closer to each other. During this approach, a visual CUE appears, signaling the opportunity to execute a counterattack, which triggers the ability system. The counterattack must consist of 2 abilities, one from each character, resulting in either damage or a buff. Depending on the combination, the counterattack can hit different weak points on the enemy, potentially dealing no damage. For the counterattack to successfully deal damage, it's necessary for the combination(s) to result in an attack on the enemy's weak point, which could be:
      - High Frontal;
      - Mid Frontal;
      - Low Frontal;
      - Back;
      - Combinations of the above types (e.g., Mid/Low Front).
    - If one of the players is hit by the enemy's attack, the counterattack is not activated, and the hit player becomes unconscious, waiting for their partner to rescue them in the next attack window. SUGGESTION: The weak point may shift as the battle progresses.
    - 
  - During Combat
    - The enemy has a maximum activation distance, allowing the player to avoid combat by staying beyond this range. If the player surpasses this distance, the enemy stops pursuing and returns to its original position.
    - 
  - Combat Resolution
    - Combat is successfully completed when the players hit the enemy's weak point a certain number of times. Combat fails when both players are unconscious simultaneously.

- Movement
  - The game's movement system is initially straightforward, utilizing the integrated 2D physics system in the Unity3D engine. However, this system is designed in a 3D environment, where the X-axis corresponds to the distance along a Bezier Spline curve. Therefore, in every frame, there is a calculation of the Vector3 position on the spline that corresponds to the X-axis position in the 2D mechanics. Consequently, no 3D objects have colliders, and no objects with colliders or rigid bodies have visual representation.
  - To simplify the assembly of scenarios and their respective colliders, some functions have been added. The first one is GenerateCollider, executed at the beginning of the level, which creates a 2D Poly Collider by using raycasts originating from the curve. This approach provides a 2D collider representing the path to be traversed by the player.
  - The Position Collider function calculates, through approximation, the position of this object in relation to the curve and its distance from the start to this curve. This number represents the X-axis in 2D.
  - Every object moving in 2D is updated in 3D using the Projector script, applying the same logic described above.

Post Mortem

- Lessons Learned
  - 1st Semester 
    - The first semester of this project focused on developing a movement system that utilized the Unity-provided foundation, aiming to create a unique aspect compared to existing options in the market. However, this approach led to some challenges as there was no clear reference for how it should be done or whether the direction was correct. This resulted in a trial-and-error cycle that consumed a significant amount of time and effort. In hindsight, it might have been simpler to work within Unity's capabilities and focus more on the game's concept, narrative, and visuals.
    - Regarding the 3D modeling adopted during this semester, the perspective gained from previous semesters was helpful, but deeper development was needed to align it with the game's requirements. The goal was to maintain simplicity by using a low-polygon approach while preserving the recognition of the created objects, achieving a visual style resembling 2D animation.
  - 2nd Semester 
    - In general, we found that the estimated time for creating elements was underestimated. The initial idea for the game grew into something complex that couldn't be fully developed by a team of four within a year. Although we were aware of this possibility, the mistake was attempting to create a complete game within that time frame instead of opting for a simpler version. While the initial execution time might be short, the adjustment phase can turn out to be much longer, both in programming (e.g., merging two scripts) and animation (e.g., refining a walk cycle for the correct sensation).
    - Moreover, taking on tasks that the developer hasn't tackled before while learning on the go can lead to various issues. Some of these challenges required redoing assets, especially in areas like rigging and 3D animation.
    - During this semester, we also began using Trello. Its simplicity made organization easier, but Trello isn't optimized for game development. Since our game's elements are interdependent, using cards for organization felt somewhat awkward and difficult to manage, albeit easier than using HacknPlan.
- What Could Have Been Done Differently
  - 1st Semester 
    - Prioritize pre-production to focus on the core essence, story, and most importantly, the visual concepts. This approach would help cover and anticipate all game features, thus creating an effective art direction. Enhance organization by utilizing available platforms better and distributing tasks more efficiently.
  - 2nd Semester 
    - Start testing the execution of certain tasks earlier to better understand the time required for each. This would provide a more realistic perception of the project's completion time and help in reducing scope. Consequently, it would lead to a more concise and manageable project plan.

Gameplay

Basic Player Movement

Player is able to move through the maze with a D-pad. The torch illuminates all the player is able to see. The character is always looking forward, so the player cannot walk backwards. The camera follows the character in a top-down view, turning with the player movement, so the selected path to go is always forward for the player.

Player Actions

Put out Torch (turn light off)

Enables the player to hide from the Minotaur if it's close to it. But also removes almost all visibility that the user can have on the labyrinth.

Light up Torch (turn light on)
> Uses one [Match](https://workflowy.com/#/bf484e81b0b9)
> 

Enables the player to see the way they are going, but makes them visible to the Minotaur.

Throw Torch

Throwing the torch enables the player to escape the minotaur, by for example throwing the torch on a path bifurcation to one way and going to another, the minotaur will follow the light and loose the player's location.

The player is also able to throw the torch to break walls.

Pick up Torch

Picking up the torch that was thrown fixes the light again to the player's character.

Enemy AI

Idle

Minotaurs basic idle state

Patrolling 

We have a smart patrolling so the game is more interesting. Minotaur patrols either to a completely random location on the maze or a location 20 units close to the player. When it arrives, it selects a new random point. 

The patrol point varies from smart (close to player) to normal at random to the Minotaur.

A Patrol point has to be accessible to the Minotaur to be valid.

If the Player is found, the Minotaur goes into the Chasing state, with a pause before entering the state, in which it [roars](https://workflowy.com/#/0a7c29f8a442) in place. If the player is not found, the Minotaur keeps patrolling

Chasing

The Minotaur found the player, and is going in its direction. The player can hide in Hidden places, throw the torch to escape, or turn it off. 

If the Minotaur gets to the player it goes into the attacking state. If it loses the player of sight, it returns to patrolling.

Attack

Attacking means the player is close enough to be reached by the attack.

The camera changes angle to show the attack from a side perspective. The UI for movement is disabled and the player has to hit the correct points on the screen to escape the Minotaurs attack

If the player succeeds the Minotaur goes into Stunned state

Stunned

The Minotaur is stunned. It doesn't move for 10 seconds. 

Meanwhile, the player has no control of Alex, which is running randomly through the labyrinth. There should show a light to the exit of the maze as well. 

When the Minotaur is no longer stunned it goes back to Idle

Win Conditions

Find the labyrinth exit

Find all [6 cards](https://workflowy.com/#/eeaa6ab52366) for [Exodia](https://workflowy.com/#/2a95948de693)

Loss Conditions

Minotaur gets Alex

When the Minotaur gets the player character, they have a chance to escape the attack with a quick time event, taping the screen where it indicates to dodge the minotaur's attack. 

If the player succeeds, they escape the attack and a light shows the labyrinth's exit path. They also loose control of the character for a few seconds and Alex runs randomly through the maze while the Minotaur is stunned.

If the player fails it is Game Over.

Fall into a trap

If the player falls into a trap, it's Game Over

Level Interactions

Traps

Only Alex can be affected by a trap. The Minotaur is immune to them.

Wall traps

Spikes come out of the walls to kill Alex!

Floor traps

The floor opens to reveal a fire pit!



Disarm Traps

Find the button corresponding with the fire or spikes color to disarm the trap

Breakable walls

Walls that the player can throw their torches into and break them

Hiding Spots

Places with a mysterious purple fog where not even the Minotaur dares to step in (it's totally safe, trust me). The player will be able to hide with the torch on in these places.



User Interface

Style 

Typography

Typography should resemble medieval to ancient Greece. they should be light, with serif and easy to read in small elements, and very structured for others, to also complement the game low poly style.

Typography selection:

For titles and big texts: [Tilt Prism](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Tilt+Prism)

For small texts and buttons: [Texturina](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Texturina)

UI Elements

The style is based on semi-realistic buttons resembling stone and energy. Although the idea is to have a simplistic UI for the game in the user experience, the style is heavy and resembles realistic elements within the fantasy world. 

Buttons

Buttons have sharp angles and uneven corners to resemble physical rock buttons. They also have borders that simulate an inward/downward motion for when active

- 
  > Button Example

D-Pad

D-Pad resembles a diamond on an energy pad that is transparent. when moving the D-pad a red halo should appear in the direction it is moved
